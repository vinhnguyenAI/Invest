## Business in two sentences
Korea FT makes carbon canisters, which trap fuel-tank vapour as emissions regulation requires, plus plastic filler necks and interior parts. It sells them to Hyundai/Kia (exclusive canister supplier) and to global OEMs from plants in Korea, China, India, Poland, Slovakia and the US. Hybrids need higher-spec, higher-priced canisters, so the mix shift to HEV/PHEV lifts both content per car and margins.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩815.2bn (TTM to 1H26)
- Normalised net margin: **4.0%**
- Normalised net income: ₩32.6bn → **normalised EY 21.9%** (P/E 4.6x) vs hurdle 8.5%
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. OPM has been 5.0–6.3% for three years (FY23–1H26), up from ~3.5% in FY22. The driver is identifiable: the HEV canister mix at Hyundai/Kia. After interest (net debt is modest) and a ~22–25% tax rate, a 5.5% OPM gives a NPM of about 4%. That matches FY2025 (~3.9%) and sits below the brokerage FY26E of 5.0%.
  2. The external backdrop is ordinary. Auto volumes are not at a peak, input-cost relief is not doing the work, and the KRW is not unusually weak. Overseas plants (China, Europe) are a drag, not a tailwind, so we are not normalising on peak conditions.
- Bear case: 2.5% NPM, back to the pre-HEV-mix margin (FY22-type 3.5% OPM), with China losses and Hyundai price-downs. That gives NI ₩20.4bn and **EY 13.7%**, still above the hurdle.

## Owner & governance
- Largest holder: **SIS S.R.L.** (Italy) with 9,719,032 shares (34.91%) at 1H26. It has held this position since 2007, and its CEO Simone Ciminelli is a non-executive inside director who has missed key board votes for 5 years (Economist KR, Sep 2026).
- Founder **오원석 회장** holds 1,173,516 shares (4.22%). The company says SIS has delegated management to him since the 2012 listing. The CEO is 김재산 사장. The owner group incl. related parties holds ~39.5%.
- VIP Asset Management holds ~13.2% (Mar 2026).
- The criterion asks for a founder actively running the company *and* a meaningful owner stake. The founder runs it, but his own economic stake is small, and control sits with a passive foreign corporate holder. That makes criterion 4 a **marginal** pass at best.
- Shareholder returns are improving: a ₩10bn buyback is in progress and the dividend has risen for 3 consecutive years.

## Growth runway
- HEV/PHEV canister content per vehicle at Hyundai/Kia, which is expanding hybrid line-ups for 2026–28.
- Overseas plants (India, Europe, US).
- Growth is slowing to high single digits (FY25 +6.8%, 1H26 +7%), and the FY26E +7% would pull the 3y CAGR below 10% next year.

## Key risks
- Customer concentration on Hyundai/Kia, and annual price-downs.
- A full BEV transition removes the canister and filler neck. Hybrids are a bridge, not a permanent home.
- Losses at the China subsidiary, and capex for the R&D building expansion.
- Control sits with a passive foreign holder (SIS). A stake sale could bring in a new owner.
- Moderate leverage. Small cap with low liquidity.
