## Business in two sentences
S&D makes customised seasoning ingredients: sauce bases, powdered soup and seasoning mixes, and K-food powders such as doenjang and kimchi. It sells them to Korean food manufacturers, which pay for consistent, recipe-specific flavour components they would otherwise have to develop and produce themselves. Its key customer is Samyang Foods, where S&D is reported to be the exclusive supplier of core ingredients for the Buldak liquid and powder sauces. Samyang's export boom (2Q26 overseas sales +46.7%) pulls S&D's volumes.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩172.5bn (TTM, verified quarterly data)
- Normalised net margin: **13.0%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩22.4bn → **normalised EY 13.3%** (P/E 7.5x) vs hurdle 8.5%. TTM actual NI of ₩24.2bn gives 14.4%.
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was 13.4%, 13.5%, 14.7% and 13.7% in FY22–FY25 and 13.6% in 1H26. It has been remarkably stable through the 2022 input-cost spike (oils, spices, packaging) and the 2024–26 volume boom. 13.0% is below every year in the series.
  2. Mechanism. As a custom B2B formulator, S&D passes raw-material costs through with a lag. Utilisation is high on Buldak volumes, and OPM has held at 15–17% even as capacity grew. The FY25 OPM dip (17.3% to 15.2%) reflects cost and capex ramp. Samyang itself runs a >20% OPM, so its ingredient suppliers are not being squeezed to break-even.
- Bear case: 10% NPM, if Samyang dual-sources or brings sauce bases in-house, or Buldak export growth stalls and fixed costs from new capacity bite → ₩17.3bn → **EY 10.2%**. That is still above the hurdle.

## Growth runway
Growth tracks Samyang's overseas Buldak expansion (1H26 Samyang revenue +37%, with new US and China capacity) plus other K-food exporters (Nongshim and others). Industry trend: K-sauce and K-ramen exports. Growth has re-accelerated, with 1H26 at +29% versus FY25's +30%. Risk: growth is largely one customer's product.

## Key risks
- **Customer concentration:** Samyang/Buldak is likely the majority of growth. Samyang could dual-source or bring production in-house.
- Fad risk in K-food exports. FX and raw-material spikes (chilli, oils).
- Capacity expansion costs ahead of demand.
- Small float and liquidity (2.9m shares).
