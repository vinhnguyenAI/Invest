## Business in two sentences
Hankuk Carbon makes the reinforced polyurethane-foam insulation panels that line the cargo tanks of membrane-type LNG carriers and sells them to the big three Korean shipyards. Its revenue is tied to LNG-carrier construction schedules 1–3 years after orders. It also sells carbon and glass-fibre composite materials (prepreg, wind and aerospace).

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩908bn (TTM ≈ FY25)
- Normalised net margin: **9%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩82bn → **normalised EY 8.4%** (P/E 11.9x) vs hurdle 8.5%
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. OPM was 7–18% over 2019–2021 (2020: ₩76bn on ₩412bn, ~18% peak), 0–7% over 2022–2024 (factory fire in 2023, low-price orders, labour and raw-material costs) and 14–21% over 2025–1H26. A mid-cycle OPM of ~11–12% gives ~9% NPM after tax.
  2. The current 19–21% OPM reflects high-priced 2022–23 LNG orders now being delivered, a weak KRW (exports and USD-linked pricing) and cost cuts. These are cyclical, not structural.
- Bear case: 6% NPM (fewer LNG orders after the 2022–24 boom, KRW strengthens, Chinese yards and panel competitors such as 동성화인텍) → ₩55bn → **EY 5.6%**.

## Owner & governance
The company is founder-family run (조문수 → 조연호), and the family group holds 35%. Governance is poor: a ₩30bn retirement payment to the founder, NPS opposing director pay and auditor items, and CBs whose call options let the family increase its stake cheaply, which dilutes minority shareholders.

## Balance sheet
The company is likely net cash (~₩313bn cash vs ~₩149bn debt; date to verify). It nonetheless keeps issuing CBs despite holding cash, which is a "habit" flag under criterion #5.

## Growth runway
The LNG-carrier backlog fell from $1.56bn (end-2023) to $1.16bn (mid-2025). New US-LNG-driven orders in 2025–26 may refill it, and Daol raised its target in Jun-2026, but 2026 revenue looks flat at ~₩0.9tn. Composites are small.

## Key risks
LNG shipbuilding cycle. Customer concentration in three shipyards. KRW appreciation. CB dilution and governance. A repeat of the 2023 fire.
