## Business in two sentences
Sena designs Bluetooth/mesh intercom headsets that clip onto motorcycle helmets (and increasingly cycling, ski and industrial helmets). It sells them through dealers, OEM helmet makers and online, mainly in North America and Europe, to riders who pay for group communication, music and navigation while riding.

- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. Gross margin is close to 50% (1Q26). FY25 OPM of 9.1% was depressed by IPO costs, new-business hiring and marketing, and 2Q26 shows 24% OPM in the peak riding season. Net margin of 10% sits between FY25 and the 1H26 run-rate.
  2. USD revenue gives an FX tailwind at the current weak KRW. Seasonality is strong (2Q/3Q peak).
- Bear case: 7% NPM (USD/KRW normalises, competition from Cardo and Chinese low-cost intercoms) → ₩12.6bn → EY ~7%.

## Owner & governance
Kakao Games bought control of Sena in the early 2020s and sold 37.6% to Keystone Partners (PE) in 2024, keeping ~16%. Keystone's stake is 33.7% after the IPO, and it has said it will not sell for 2.5 years. Inews24 flagged a 26% overhang of old shares on listing day and criticised the IPO price as unfairly high compared with the PE's buy-in price. A PE exit is the eventual endgame. This is not an owner-operator.

## Growth runway
The core motorcycle market is mature, but Sena is expanding into the North American OEM helmet channel (a US$15m North America push), plus outdoor and industrial products. 2026 looks like a strong growth year.

## Key risks
PE exit overhang. Consumer-discretionary cyclicality. Competition from Cardo and Chinese brands. FX. Seasonality.
