## Business in two sentences
T&L makes hydrocolloid patches (acne "pimple patches" and wound dressings) and microneedle patches on its own lines in Korea. It sells them mostly to US brand owners, chiefly Hero/Church & Dwight's Mighty Patch, which pay for a medically certified, high-volume, low-cost supplier. Domestic hospital and pharmacy dressings and a growing Europe channel make up the rest.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ~₩177bn (TTM, estimate)
- Normalised net margin: **25%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩44.3bn → **normalised EY 10.7%** (P/E 9.3x) vs hurdle 8.5%
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was ~25.7%, 23.7%, 26.5% and 23.2% in FY22–FY25 (avg ~24.8%). 25% sits at the 4-year average and well below the 1H26 run-rate (~34% NPM on 43% OPM).
  2. Drivers. 1H26 margins were inflated by the reversal of the 2025 inventory-policy change (stock moved from C&D to T&L and normalised from March 2026), plus high utilisation and FX. The FY25 trough reflected that destocking and sharing US tariffs with the customer. Normal conditions sit between the two.
- Bear case: 20% NPM on FY25-level revenue of ₩164bn (Church & Dwight squeezes price, adds second-source ODMs, tariffs stick) → ₩32.8bn → **EY 7.9%**, below the hurdle.

## Growth runway
Mighty Patch's geographic expansion under Church & Dwight (Europe ~5% of 1Q26 but growing fast), new body-area patches, microneedles. Kiwoom forecasts FY26 revenue of ₩200bn (+22%) and OP of ₩75.6bn. The FY25 decline shows how dependent sales are on one customer's inventory decisions.

## Key risks
Single-customer concentration (Hero/Church & Dwight). US tariffs. Low barriers to entry for patch ODMs from China and Korea. Margin mean-reversion from the record 1H26 level. Share-count discrepancy to check.
