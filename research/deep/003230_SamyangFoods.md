## Business in two sentences
Samyang Foods makes Buldak, a super-spicy instant-noodle brand, along with sauces and other ramen. It sells mainly to overseas distributors and retailers (US Walmart/Costco, China, SE Asia, Europe), and consumers pay for a viral, differentiated flavour at a premium to ordinary ramen. Overseas sales passed ₩600bn in a single quarter for the first time in 2Q26.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩2,754bn (TTM)
- Normalised net margin: **17%**
- Normalised net income: ₩468bn → **normalised EY 5.4%** (P/E 18.4x) vs hurdle 8.5%.
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM rose from ~9–11% (FY22–23) to 15.7% (FY24), 16.5% (FY25) and 19% (1H26), driven by export mix and price premium. OPM has been 20%+ for six straight quarters. 17% sits between FY25 and 1H26.
  2. Drivers. Input costs (flour, palm oil) and a weak KRW currently help. A stronger KRW or commodity spike would cut margins. Even at the 1H26 peak of 19% NPM the EY is only 6.1%.
- Bear case: 14% NPM (KRW appreciation, US tariffs, competing spicy copycats) → ₩386bn → **EY 4.5%**.

## Owner & governance
This is a classic family company. Chairman 전인장 and his wife, CEO 김정수, run it, and their son 전병우 is an executive. The family controls it through the unlisted holding company 삼양라운드스퀘어. The chairman's 2019 embezzlement conviction is a governance blemish. Stake details are from memory.

## Balance sheet
Not verified this round. From memory, net debt is modest and falling fast thanks to ₩500bn+ of annual OP. Capex for the Miryang 2nd plant and the Chinese plant is being funded internally.

## Growth runway
Strong: US mainstream retail distribution, new capacity coming online and Europe expansion. The risk is that a single brand and fad drives everything.

## Key risks
Valuation. Single-brand concentration. KRW appreciation. US tariffs. Food-safety and regulatory recalls (e.g. Denmark 2024).
