# Kumhwa PSC (036190): can it get off coal? Synthesis (2026-10-16)

Based on three parallel research passes: [01 company strategy](01_company_strategy.md), [02 policy & competition](02_policy_competition.md) and [03 coal exposure](03_coal_exposure.md). All facts come from web-search snippets; DART filings, broker PDFs and Naver were blocked. Verify the key items on DART.

## Short answer
**There is no announced plan to move off coal, but a partial, quiet shift into nuclear is under way.** The shift is real, yet it is smaller than the coal pool it has to replace. The main replacement market, new large gas power plants, now looks partly closed to Kumhwa because of a July 2026 government-labour agreement.

## What Kumhwa has actually done
- **Water treatment and nuclear entry:** bought 에코비트엔지니어링 (now **금화정수**) for ₩85bn in Dec-2022. The stated aim was to enter nuclear maintenance and reduce reliance on thermal plants (Dealsite, Oct-2022). 금화정수 is about 14% of revenue at roughly 5% operating margin (derived).
- **Nuclear share is rising:** nuclear went from 6.3% of the parent company's revenue (2020) to 15.7% (2022) to 21.4% (2023) (KIRS 2024-07; eBest 2023). The work is on peripheral systems, not the reactor itself.
- **Largest recent contract is nuclear, not coal:** ₩58.9bn with Hyundai E&C for auxiliary and HVAC installation at the Shin-Hanul 3·4 nuclear units, Sep-2025 to Apr-2031 (disclosed 2025-08-27).
- **Not found:** CEO interviews, IR or AGM strategy statements, and any activity in offshore wind, solar, hydrogen or ammonia, small modular reactors, decommissioning, data centres or overseas maintenance. A possible ₩26.6bn overseas nuclear contract ("HSJV") is unconfirmed.

## How much is at stake (estimates)
- **Coal-linked revenue today:** about **45% of group revenue** (range 37–55%), roughly ₩190bn, and about 50–60% of operating profit. Arithmetic: parent revenue ₩320bn × ~75% thermal × ~80% of thermal is coal (the 80% is an assumption; DART has the real split).
- **Revenue at risk, net of some sites kept through conversion:** about 5% by 2030, 10% by 2036 (11th Plan pace), up to about 19% by 2036 on the 2040 full-exit path. This assumes Kumhwa keeps 40% of converted sites; if the July deal holds, that figure is optimistic.
- **Pace:** a drag of about 1–2% of revenue a year. Nuclear and water growth still outweigh it today (1H26 revenue +12%), but likely not after the early 2030s unless more nuclear work is won.

## What this means for the investment case
- **Valuation still has a buffer.** At ₩193bn market cap and ~₩30bn net income (EY ~15%), plus an estimated ₩80–110bn net cash, the price already implies decline. Even if the coal-linked half of profit disappeared by the late 2030s, the remaining nuclear and water business plus cash could still be worth roughly today's price.
- **But this is a shrinking business with a nuclear side-bet, not a growth compounder.** Criterion 6 (growth phase) holds near-term, but it looks fragile beyond about 2030.
- **Revised verdict: WATCH (downgraded on durability).** It suits someone happy with a cheap, cash-rich, high-dividend (~4.7% yield) company with a defined runway. It does not suit someone requiring a long growth phase.

## What would change the view
1. **Positive:** a FY2025 사업보고서 fuel split showing coal below 35% of revenue; new nuclear maintenance contracts (for example 2027+ new-build units, KHNP outsourcing); private plants and combined heat-and-power plants confirmed as still open to tender; buybacks or special dividends from the cash pile.
2. **Negative:** official confirmation of the ≥200 MW clause; loss of coal contracts at re-tender after the 2027 merger; cash spent on an unrelated acquisition.
