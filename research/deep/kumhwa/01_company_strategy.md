The target treats water and wastewater and does O&M on water-treatment equipment at **nuclear and thermal** plants. Dealsite states the purpose was to *enter nuclear 경상정비 and diversify away from a revenue base concentrated on thermal-plant 경상정비*. Source: https://dealsite.co.kr/articles/93683 (Oct-2022).

Sources: KIRS 2024-07-22 (link above); eBest 2023-04-25, https://file.alphasquare.co.kr/media/pdfs/company-report/이베스트20230425금화피에스시.pdf ; eBest 2023-05-16, https://file.alphasquare.co.kr/media/pdfs/company-report/이베스트20230516금화피에스시.pdf

## (b) Announced plans / stated strategy
- **[PLAN] Diversification into nuclear via 금화정수** is the only explicit, sourced statement of strategic intent I found (Dealsite, Oct-2022). Analysts (eBest 2023, KIRS 2024) list "nuclear maintenance revenue growth + 금화정수" as the investment points.
- I found **no CEO interviews** (김성기 or 김경태), IR decks or AGM remarks that state a coal-exit or new-energy strategy. The company has a low media profile.
- I found **nothing** on: offshore wind or solar O&M, hydrogen or ammonia co-firing, SMR, decommissioning, data centres, overseas (Middle East or SE Asia) O&M, new licences, JVs or MOUs. Searches for each came back empty. The company does not appear in the Barakah maintenance deals (those went to KHNP-KPS and Doosan).

## (c) Speculation / inference
- **[SPEC]** "HSJV" may be the Hyundai E&C–Samsung C&T JV, a pattern used on overseas nuclear builds. That is unverified.
- **[SPEC] What happens on a coal-to-LNG conversion is not documented anywhere I found.** Government plans convert most retiring coal units into LNG combined-cycle units on the *same sites*: the 9th Basic Plan retires 30 coal units by 2034 and converts 24 to LNG, for example Hadong (Namdong/Nambu). Recent cases include Hadong 2·3 → LNG CC, with an HRSG contract signed Sep-2026 (https://www.fnnews.com/news/202609021013275526 ; https://futurechosun.com/?p=52955 ; https://m.ekn.kr/view.php?key=474691). Genco 경상정비 contracts are re-tendered competitively, so an incumbent has a site and workforce advantage but **no contractual right to keep the site**. A CC plant also needs fewer maintenance staff than a coal plant: no coal handling, ash or FGD. Expect some volume loss per site even when the site is kept. MOTIE's 2024 line ("LNG 대체 불가, 무탄소 전원 전환", https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202401291546001) adds the risk that some coal sites are not converted to LNG at all.
- **[SPEC]** The 1H26 revenue rise (+12%) with lower margins is consistent with lower-margin construction work, such as Shin-Hanul 3·4, filling in.

## Conclusion
1. Kumhwa has no articulated, public "coal exit" plan: no CEO statement, IR roadmap, or renewables, hydrogen or overseas initiative was found.
2. Its de-facto pivot is **nuclear**. The nuclear share of parent revenue went from 6% (2020) to 21% (2023). It bought 금화정수 for ₩85bn (Dec-2022), which covers nuclear and thermal water treatment and is ~14% of group revenue. It won the ₩58.9bn Shin-Hanul 3·4 contract (2025–31) and a ₩26.6bn overseas nuclear job.
3. LNG combined-cycle maintenance is already part of its thermal base, so a coal-to-LNG conversion on the same site is a re-tender opportunity, not a guaranteed loss or a guaranteed keep. It does mean smaller maintenance scopes.
4. The pivot is incremental and opportunistic, following Korea's nuclear revival. It is not a transformational diversification. Coal is probably still the largest single exposure; the exact share is unverified and needs the 사업보고서 segment table.
