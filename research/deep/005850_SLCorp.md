## Business in two sentences
SL designs and makes automotive lamps (mainly LED headlamps and rear combination lamps), plus mirrors and some electronic modules, and sells them to carmakers, chiefly Hyundai/Kia and GM. OEMs buy from SL because a lamp is a vehicle-specific, safety-certified part sourced for the life of a model, and switching suppliers mid-cycle is costly.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩5,394bn (TTM to 1Q26, calculated)
- Normalised net margin: **5.5%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩296.7bn → **normalised EY 13.0%** (P/E 7.7x) vs hurdle 8.5%
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. OPM went from 4.7% (FY22, chip shortage and raw-material spike) to ~10–11% (1H24, peak LED mix), 7.8% (FY25, US tariffs and India competition) and 10.3% (1Q26). A through-cycle OPM of ~7–8% after ~20–25% tax and small minorities gives ~5.5% NPM, below FY25's 6.1% and well below 1Q26's 9.8%.
  2. Drivers. Full LED adoption on new Hyundai/Kia models and a growing GM share lift the content per vehicle. Mexico and India plants are ramping (utilisation upside). Tariff costs are partly being passed through. Peers: Hyundai Mobis lamp division and global lamp makers (Koito ~4–6% NPM) support 4–6%.
- Bear case: 3.5% NPM (FY22-like squeeze, sustained US tariffs, India price war) → ₩188.8bn → **EY 8.3%**, just below the hurdle.

## Balance sheet
Unverified this pass. My memory is that SL holds sizeable cash and financial assets against modest borrowings (net cash) and has 부채비율 <100%. Confirm on DART. No CB/BW or rights issues found.

## Growth runway
2023 order intake was a record ₩2.03tn, and the 2026 order target is ₩1.6tn (30% achieved by April). The 2026E consensus is revenue ₩5.88tn (+12.3%) and OP ₩512bn (Hanwha). Drivers are GM and Hyundai US volumes, Mexico/India ramps and electronics (BMS/SBCM). The risk is that the 3-year record (7.9% CAGR) is below the bar, so 2026 must deliver ≥10%.

## Key risks
US tariffs on Korean and Mexican parts. Hyundai/Kia concentration. India competition. Succession and related-party dealings. Auto-cycle downturn. FX (a strong KRW hurts translation of overseas plants).
