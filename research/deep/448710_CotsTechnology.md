## Business in two sentences
COTS Technology designs and builds military-grade embedded computers: single-board computers, mission and fire-control computers, and rugged displays. It sells them to Korean defence primes (Hanwha Aerospace, LIG Nex1, Hyundai Rotem, KAI type) for platforms such as K9, K2, Chunmoo and naval and air systems. Once a unit is qualified into a weapons system, it is bought for the life of the production run and for spares. K-defence export programmes drive volume.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩68.1bn (FY2025; TTM unavailable)
- Normalised net margin: **10%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩6.8bn → **normalised EY 11.2%** (P/E 8.9x) vs hurdle 8.5%.
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was ~13%, 12.6% and 10.9% in FY23–25. 10% is below every post-IPO year. FY22's 6.7% OPM came before the IPO and at a smaller scale.
  2. Drivers. Margin depends on mix (high-margin designed-in SBCs versus lower-margin display and assembly work) and on component costs (imported CPUs and FPGAs, FX). The FY25 gross-margin squeeze from 21% to 18% came from cost of goods, so it is partly cyclical. Interest income on IPO cash and a low effective tax rate for an SME support net margin. Eugene's FY26E OPM of 13.3% (OP ₩10.0bn on ₩75.4bn) implies ≥10% NPM.
- Bear case: **7%** (GPM stays ~18%, a K-defence export pause, programme delays) → ₩4.8bn → **EY 7.8%**, below the hurdle.

## Balance sheet
The company has been cash-generative since the IPO, and no debt issues or CBs were found. Small-cap defence suppliers carry inventory and receivables that swing with programme timing. *Net cash and 부채비율 are unverified; check DART.*

## Growth runway
Seven consecutive years of revenue growth to a record FY25, but growth slowed sharply in 2H25. The runway rests on K9, K2 and Chunmoo export programmes (Poland, Middle East), new platforms (KF-21, naval) and domestic upgrades. Eugene's FY26 forecast is +10.7% revenue and +20.8% OP. If 1H26 shows ≥10% growth, the stock would move toward PASS; if not, it fails #6.

## Key risks
Customer concentration among a few primes. Defence programme timing and lumpiness. Component costs (CPUs, FPGAs) and FX. Very small cap and thin liquidity. Growth has decelerated. The bear-case EY is below the hurdle.
