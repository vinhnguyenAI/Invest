## Business in two sentences
JS Corporation makes handbags on contract for US and European fashion brands, and through its 100% subsidiary 약진통상 makes apparel for global brands and big-box retailers, in Asian factories. It also co-owns and consolidates the Grand Hyatt Seoul hotel (operator 서울미라마), which it bought into in 2023 alongside 약진통상.

- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was 6.6% / 8.7% / 6.7% / 5.4% (FY22–1H26), averaging ~6.5% excluding FY23 one-offs. OEM OPM has been stable at 8–11%. Below the operating line, hotel interest of roughly ₩20–30bn a year (on ~₩350bn+ of debt) absorbs part of the profit, which is why 5% is used rather than the 6.5% average.
  2. Drivers. Brand order cycles (Tapestry and similar) and US retail destocking set the cycle (FY23 −11.5%). USD revenue against KRW-based reporting helps.
- Bear case: 3.0% NPM (an OEM order downcycle plus higher hotel rates and refinancing costs) → ₩43.2bn → **EY 12.4%**.

## Owner & governance
Founder 홍재성 founded the company in 1987 as a handbag OEM. He is chairman and was re-registered as 대표이사 in March 2025. With related parties he holds 57.76%. The hotel deal was described by the company as an "investment, not an acquisition". A May 2026 newsspace piece says the company declined to answer questions on the hotel's ₩348bn of maturities, ₩6.9bn of royalties and a ₩16bn owner dividend. That is a governance flag.

## Balance sheet
FY2024 consolidated figures were total assets ₩1,830.5bn, liabilities ₩1,323.1bn and equity ₩507.4bn, so 부채비율 was ~261%. Net debt is unverified but clearly large, given the hotel's ₩348bn of long-term borrowings, a ₩362bn loan converted to equity, and debt guarantees for 약진통상. No CB/rights issues were found. This fails criterion #5.

## Growth runway
Apparel OEM wins with global brands and big-box retailers drove 1Q25 +41.6% and 2Q26 +42.9%. Handbags depend on a few US brands. The hotel adds revenue but not growth quality.

## Key risks
Leverage and refinancing at the hotel. Customer concentration (a few fashion brands). US tariffs on Vietnam and Indonesia production. Related-party flows (hotel, owner dividends). Minority-interest and attributable-NI ambiguity.
