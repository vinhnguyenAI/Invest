## Business in two sentences
PharmaResearch makes polynucleotide (PN/PDRN, salmon-DNA-derived) "skin booster" injectables under the Rejuran brand and sells them to dermatology and aesthetic clinics in Korea and, increasingly, abroad. It also sells Rejuran-branded home-care cosmetics. Clinics pay because patients ask for the brand for skin-quality treatments that are repeated every few months.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩602.9bn (TTM)
- Normalised net margin: **30%**
- Normalised net income: ₩180.9bn → **normalised EY 6.4%** (P/E 15.6x) vs hurdle 8.5%. On TTM NI (₩197.6bn) the EY is 7.0%.
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was ~21% (FY22), ~25% (FY24), 31.8% (FY25) and 32.8% (TTM), while OPM ran 34–40%. Operating leverage and a richer export mix have lifted margins. 30% sits just below the current run-rate because 2Q26 OPM (37%) is already below FY25 (40%) as overseas marketing and cosmetics mix weigh on it.
  2. Peers. Hugel, Classys and other Korean aesthetics names earn 25–35% NPM at scale. Even at the peak 33% NPM, the EY stays below 8.5% (7.0%).
- Bear case: 25% NPM (more PN/PDRN competition in Korea, export ASP pressure, rising cosmetics share) → ₩150.7bn → **EY 5.4%**.
- To reach 8.5% at a 30% margin, revenue would need to be ~₩800bn, about 1.3x TTM. That is plausible by FY27 at the current growth rate, but the test uses current revenue.

## Growth runway
The runway is credible. Exports were 44% of 1H26 sales and grew +47% YoY, helped by overseas Rejuran approvals and distribution. Domestic medical tourism is also strong. This is a multi-year runway, not a one-off spike.

## Key risks
