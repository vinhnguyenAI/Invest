## Business in two sentences
Jeryong Industrial makes hardware that holds transmission and distribution lines in place: clamps, fittings, spacers and polymer insulators, including new HVDC overhead-line fittings. Its customer is KEPCO, which buys through cooperative tenders (e.g. the ₩9.0bn HVDC fittings contract in Aug-2025, equal to 30.7% of FY24 sales), and pays for reliable grid hardware.

## Normalised earnings
- Revenue base used: ₩50.7bn (TTM, calc)
- Normalised net margin: **20%** (estimate)
- Normalised net income: ₩10.1bn → **normalised EY 7.2%** (P/E 13.9x) vs hurdle 8.5%
- **Why this margin is "normal":**
  1. History. NPM was 15–21% in FY22–FY24, a period that included both a high-volume year (FY22) and a trough (FY24). The FY25/TTM 30–40% reflects operating leverage on a KEPCO order surge plus non-operating income from the cash pile. That income makes up a large share of net income because net income has exceeded operating profit in 3 of 4 years.
  2. Drivers. KEPCO's grid-expansion and HVDC programmes (the West Coast HVDC 'energy highway') support volumes, but pricing is set by tender and KEPCO capex is politically and financially cyclical. FY22→FY24 revenue halved when KEPCO cut capex under its own losses. 20% applied to the boom-level TTM revenue is already generous.
- Bear case: 14% NPM, with KEPCO capex normalising and copper/aluminium cost pass-through lagging → ₩7.1bn → **EY 5.0%**.

## Owner & governance
박종태 is CEO and the largest shareholder (22.38%, 42.13% with related parties; etfshopping, verify on DART). The company was spun off in 2011 from 경인전선개발 (the predecessor of 제룡전기), and both companies sit in the same family sphere.

## Balance sheet
At FY24 total assets were ₩84.4bn, liabilities ₩5.8bn and equity ₩78.6bn (news report, verified). Equity is about 56% of market cap and is largely cash and financial assets (estimate). There is no sign of CB/BW issuance.

## Growth runway
HVDC fittings and the grid-reinforcement push could keep FY26 strong (1H26 OP of ₩10.6bn already equals FY25 OP). However, the business is a KEPCO-capex derivative. Revenue in FY26E (~₩55–60bn) only gets back to the FY22 level, so this is not structural growth.

## Key risks
Single customer (KEPCO) and tender pricing. KEPCO's financial stress cutting capex (as in FY23–24). Metal input costs. Small scale. Results depend on lumpy orders.
